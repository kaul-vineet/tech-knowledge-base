# Ethics: classroom data, children's privacy, e-waste and accessibility — explained

## The big picture
The L400 labs and the capstone **Capstone: classroom environmental monitor network** built a system that *works*. This lesson asks whether a particular extension of it *should* be built, and how to argue that with the same discipline as an ADR: facts, options, evidence, decision, fitness check. Three earlier labs feed the argument directly. **Power budgeting (modelled, not measured)** supplies the battery numbers for the e-waste question. **Local AI at the edge gateway: it explains, code decides** supplies the rule "code decides what happens; nothing acts just in case" — here applied to what gets *collected*. And **Data logging** showed how easily a sensor becomes a per-second record.

The case: a school puts a micro:bit on each of 30 desks, logging temperature, light and **microphone sound level every second**, tagged with the desk number; a seating plan links desks to pupils. A vendor offers a free cloud dashboard with indefinite retention and a per-desk "engagement score". Boards run lesson 14's always-on logger on 2 × AAA. Two blind pupils and one with limited fine motor control are asked to "check the LEDs and press A".

The intuition in one line: **a harmless sensor reading becomes personal data the moment it can be joined to a person**, and design choices (identifiers, rate, retention, power, interaction) decide whether that happens.

## Worked analysis: the 7-step structured case analysis
The framework is the lesson's 7-step structured case analysis; step 4 uses the **ACM Code of Ethics (2018)** as the professional lens, with UK and Indian law at policy level (not legal advice).

1. **Facts and decision.** Decide: approve, modify or reject the per-desk extension. *Why first:* without a decision, an ethics discussion drifts into opinions about technology in general.
2. **Stakeholders.** Pupils (under 18, the data subjects), parents, teachers, school leadership (controller / data fiduciary), the DPO, the vendor (processor or a controller in its own right?), disabled pupils and the SEN coordinator, site staff who handle batteries, regulators (ICO, Data Protection Board of India). *Why:* the pupils bear the risk but did not choose the design.
3. **Data inventory.** For each field: identifiability, purpose, retention.
   - Desk id + seating plan → identifies a pupil.
   - Sound level per second per desk → can reveal *who talks and when*: behavioural data about a child.
   - Temperature and light per desk → personal only through the same desk link.
   - "Engagement score" → profiling of children.
   *Why:* the problem is not one field but the **join**.
4. **Obligations.** ACM 1.2 (avoid harm, including from aggregation) and 1.6 (respect privacy). The ICO Children's code standards 2 (DPIA first), 7 (high-privacy defaults), 8 (data minimisation), 12 (profiling off by default) and 14 (connected devices) all point against per-desk sound and the score. Under India's DPDP Act s.9, a fiduciary needs verifiable parental consent, and s.9(3) prohibits behavioural monitoring of children — a prohibition that consent does not cure. Indefinite vendor retention conflicts with storage limitation.
5. **Options.** (a) As proposed. (b) Room level only: the gateway averages temperature and light over the room every 10 minutes; no sound, no desk ids, 30-day retention, no score. (c) Sound only as a room-level "too loud" flag computed on the board and never stored. (d) Do not deploy.
6. **Choice with residual risk.** (b) + (c): it answers the stated purpose — is the room comfortable? — with low residual risk; reject the score. Batteries and accessibility are part of the choice:

   **See it in numbers** (problem 26.4, recomputed by `research/l500_checks.py`):
   ```
   6,552 h
   6,552/41.7 = 157.1
   158 sets = 316 cells
   9,480 cells for 30 boards
   6,552/2,158.3 = 3.04
   4 sets = 8 cells
   240 cells for 30 boards
   ```
   A 39-week year is 6,552 h. Lesson 14's always-on logger empties a set every 41.7 h: 158 sets per board, 9,480 cells for the class. The adaptive design needs 4 sets per board, 240 cells — or use USB power. These are model estimates; the ~40× ratio is the robust conclusion. Collect dead cells and boards through a WEEE-compliant route; reuse boards across cohorts.

   Accessibility: replace "check the LEDs" with sound plus serial text a screen reader can read; replace "press A" with an external large switch on the edge connector or a timetable start; use USB transfer, not Bluetooth pairing (pairing needs sight).
7. **Measure.** Count fields leaving the gateway (target: 2), audit retention, and ask the disabled pupils whether the activity worked for them. *Why:* like a fitness function, it turns the decision into something checkable.

## A contrasting view
A **consequentialist** school leader might argue: per-desk data could find a cold corner, or a pupil who is struggling, and the benefits to learning outweigh a small privacy cost; the vendor's service is free, so the budget goes to teaching. This deserves a serious answer, not dismissal. Two replies. First, on its own terms: the stated benefit (room comfort) is fully delivered by option (b); the extra benefit of the score is unproven, while the harms — chilling effects on children who know their talking is measured, a long-lived profile held by a third party — are plausible and hard to undo. "Free" usually means the data is the payment. Second, a **rights-based** view rejects the trade-off framing for children: DPDP s.9(3) and the Children's code treat certain monitoring as off-limits regardless of the benefit, much as lesson 21 refused to let a model act however helpful its actions looked.

## Worked problems
**26.4 — batteries per year.** (1) 39 × 7 × 24 = 6,552 h — *why:* continuous logging, whole weeks. (2) 6,552/41.7 = 157.1 → round **up** to 158 sets — *why:* a part-used set must still be bought. (3) × 2 cells × 30 boards = 9,480 cells. (4) Adaptive: 3.04 → 4 sets, 240 cells.

**26.3 — DPDP s.9 in three duties.** (1) Verifiable parental consent before processing (s.9(1)). (2) No processing likely to harm a child's well-being (s.9(2)). (3) No tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children (s.9(3)). *Why duty 3 is different:* it is a prohibition, not a consent condition, so a signed form does not lift it.

## Common confusions
**Q:** Temperature is never personal data. — **A:** It is not on its own; a desk id plus seating plan makes it personal.

**Q:** Parental consent fixes everything. — **A:** Not s.9(3) monitoring, and not over-collection (ICO standard 8).

**Q:** E-waste is too small to matter for one class. — **A:** 9,480 vs 240 cells is a design choice, not a rounding error.
