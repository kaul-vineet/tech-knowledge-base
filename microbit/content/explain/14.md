# Power budgeting (modelled, not measured) — explained

## The intuition
Battery life is one division: **capacity ÷ average current**. Two AAA cells hold about 1,250 mAh. The *average* mixes two very different numbers by time: the board **awake** (budgeted at 30 mA, a worst-case figure) and the board in **Deep Sleep** (assumed 0.5 mA floor for the whole board). Awake 100 % of the time averages 30 mA; awake 0.27 % averages about 0.58 mA — a 50× difference from *duty cycle* alone.

The lab pulls both levers — deep sleep between samples and **adaptive sampling**: start at 100 ms, double the interval while the value is steady, cap at 8 s, snap back to 100 ms on a change of ≥ 1 °C. The cost: an event shorter than the interval can fall between samples, unlogged.

Everything here is a **model**. The stub's `power.deep_sleep()` only moves the clock; `budget.py` turns awake time into current using labelled figures you can change.

**Analogy:** cloud cost — an always-on VM versus a function that wakes per event. Where the analogy breaks: the "idle bill" (sleep floor) is a hardware property you must measure; 0.5 vs 0.05 mA changes the answer by more than 4×.

```
avg_mA = (awake_ms × 30 + asleep_ms × SLEEP_MA) / total_ms
life_h = 1250 / avg_mA

interval: 100 →200 →400 →…→6400 →8000 (steady)   change ≥1 °C → 100
          [wake 20 ms | log | deep_sleep(interval)] ...
```

The one thing to remember: average current is set by how long you are awake, and the sleep floor is an assumption until measured.

## Walkthrough: what actually happens when you run it
1. **You run** a peek on `next_interval()` and the arithmetic for 483 wake-ups.
   **What you see**
   ```
   steady intervals: [200, 400, 800, 1600, 3200, 6400, 8000, 8000, 8000] -> on change: 100
   awake ms: 9660 avg mA: 0.5792 life h: 2158.3
   ```
   **What it means** 483 × 20 ms = 9,660 ms awake in 3,600,000 ms. That 0.27 % at 30 mA plus 99.73 % at 0.5 mA gives 0.5792 mA.

### Failure lab: inject → symptom → diagnose → fix → proof
2. **Inject.** **You run** `python3 budget.py`: each design runs one simulated hour against a fixed temperature trace (20 °C, a rise to 24 °C around minute 10, back to 20 °C at minute 40).
   **What you see**
   ```
   {"design": "main.py", "samples": 36000, "wakeups": 0, "max_delay_ms": 0, "meets_brief": false, "awake_pct": 100.0, "avg_ma": 30.0, "life_h": 41.7, …}
   {"design": "solution.py", "samples": 483, "wakeups": 483, "max_delay_ms": 4700, "meets_brief": true, "awake_pct": 0.27, "avg_ma": 0.5792, "life_h": 2158.3, "life_h_range": [2158.3, 9588.4], "chip_only_bound_h": 15244.7}
   ```
3. **Symptom.** `main.py` samples every 100 ms (36,000 per hour) with `sleep()`, which keeps the board on: 1250 / 30 = 41.7 h — under two days against a 720 h (30-day) brief. `test_fault` pins it (`Ran 1 test … OK`).
4. **Diagnose.** The board never sleeps and samples steady air 10 times a second.
5. **Fix.** `solution.py`: adaptive interval + `power.deep_sleep(ms=interval)`.
6. **Proof.** **You run** the build-task tests on both files.
   **What you see**
   ```
   AssertionError: 0 != 600
   AssertionError: True is not false : the spike was sampled
   FAILED (failures=3, errors=1)
   Ran 4 tests in 0.042s
   ```
   **What it means** `main.py` never deep-sleeps (0 sleeps where 600 were expected) and, sampling constantly, catches a 1.5 s spike the solution is *expected* to miss — the test documents the trade-off. `solution.py` passes all 4.

7. **Read the range.** 2,158.3 h is the board estimate at the conservative 0.5 mA floor; 9,588.4 h at an optimistic 0.05 mA; 15,244.7 h is the nRF52833 chip alone and is **never** a board figure. Worst detection delay 4,700 ms is inside the 10 s brief.

8. **You run** `python3 try_it.py`.
   **What you see**
   ```
   try 1: solution.py: avg 6.4 mA, life 195.3 h, meets brief False
   try 2: main.py: avg 7.9 mA, life 158.2 h, meets brief False
   ```
   **What it means** Deep Sleep at a fixed 100 ms rate is still 20 ms awake per 120 ms slot — the *rate* decides. And even the optimistic chip-only awake figure (7.9 mA) misses the brief for the always-on design.

Expected (not run here): on a real board the sleep floor, wake-up time and display current must be measured; whether the simulator emulates `power.deep_sleep` is unverified.

## Common confusions
**Q:** Deep Sleep alone gives a month. — **A:** Try 1: 195.3 h at a fixed 100 ms.

**Q:** 15,244.7 h is the battery life. — **A:** That is `chip_only_bound_h`, a chip-only bound.

**Q:** Adaptive sampling is free. — **A:** The spike test shows a short event can be missed.
