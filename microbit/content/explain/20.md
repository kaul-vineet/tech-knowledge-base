# A digital twin of the sensor network — explained

## The intuition
A **digital twin** here is a small statistical model of what each board *should* report. Calibrated on the first 6 windows of 30 readings, it predicts per board the temperature and the **retry share** (fraction of readings retransmitted, which tracks link loss). Each new window becomes a **z-score**: how many model standard deviations from the prediction?

The hard part is deciding *when z means drift*. Noise alone puts a window beyond 2σ about 1 time in 20 — with six boards × two signals, a drizzle of false alarms nobody reads. Real drift is **small but persistent**, and **CUSUM** (cumulative sum) exploits that. It keeps `S_hi = max(0, S_hi + z − K)`: every window subtracts a slack `K`, so random ups and downs drain back to 0, while a sustained shift larger than K keeps adding up until it crosses a limit `H`. A mirror `S_lo` catches downward drift.

**Analogy:** an SLO burn-rate alert rather than a threshold on one latency sample. Where the analogy breaks: the twin assumes boards are independent, but on a shared relay one failing link raises its neighbours' retries — so alarms are hypotheses, never actions.

```
gateway rows ─► window of 30 per (dev, signal) ─► twin predicts μ, σ ─► z = (x̄ − μ)/σ
   A/B:  |z| > 2 (or 3) ─► alarm                  (memoryless: each window alone)
   C:    S_hi = max(0, S_hi + z − 0.75) ─► S_hi > 5 ─► latched alarm   (memory: evidence accumulates)
```

The one thing to remember: a single window is noise; drift is the *sum* of small deviations, so accumulate evidence before you alarm.

## Walkthrough: what actually happens when you run it
1. **You run** a peek — hand-made z-values, not lab data — through CUSUM.
   **What you see**
   ```
   nominal z  : [0.4, -1.1, 2.3, -0.5, 0.9, -2.1, 1.2, 0.3]
     S_hi K=.75: [0.0, 0.0, 1.55, 0.3, 0.45, 0.0, 0.45, 0.0] | |z|>2 fires at [2, 5]
   shifted z  : [1.6, 1.4, 1.9, 1.2, 1.7, 1.5, 1.8, 1.3]
     S_hi K=.75: [0.85, 1.5, 2.65, 3.1, 4.05, 4.8, 5.85, 6.4] | first > H at window 6
     S_hi K=0  : [1.6, 3.0, 4.9, 6.1, 7.8, 9.3, 11.1, 12.4] | first > H at window 3
   ```
   **What it means** The 2σ rule fires twice on noise; CUSUM peaks at 1.55 and drains. On a steady +1.5σ shift no window passes 2, yet CUSUM crosses 5 at window 6; `K = 0` is faster but lets noise accumulate too.

   ```
   AssertionError: 13 not less than or equal to 2
   ```

   **What you see**

   **What it means** The twin's timer constants match lab 17's code, and calibration recovers each link's loss within 0.07.

### Why the ADR chose C, and how it stays true
5. ADR 0020 picks CUSUM with K = 0.75 (the textbook 0.5 gave up to 7 false alarms per 1,000 on the skewed retry share) and compares a retry *share*, not mean latency, because one 2 s outlier moves a window mean by 5σ or more.
6. **You run** `python3 -m unittest test_fitness`.
   **What you see**
   ```
   Ran 2 tests in 3.711s
   ```
   **What it means** ≤ 5 alarms per 1,000 nominal windows at 4 % and 10 % loss (3 seeds each), and both drifts within 10 windows.

7. **You run** `python3 try_it.py`.
   **What you see**

   ```
   try 2: {"n3 DRIFT_TEMP": 5} | false alarms: fixture 6, synthetic 74 per 1000
   ```
   **What it means** `K = 0` detects n3 in 5 windows but gives 74 false alarms per 1,000; n5's alarm latched *before* its drift began.

## Common confusions
**Q:** A tighter z-limit fixes it. — **A:** `|z| > 3`: still 12 per 1,000, and slower.

**Q:** CUSUM alarms are faults. — **A:** Two run alarms were a real burst and a coupling the model ignores.

**Q:** `K = 0` is just faster. — **A:** Try 2: 74 false alarms per 1,000.
