# Backpressure and load shedding: bounded queues, 503 + Retry-After, AIMD concurrency limits — explained

## The intuition
4 workers at 10 ms per request finish 400 requests/s, no matter what. Extra arrivals wait in a queue: throughput is capped, waiting is not. Little's law: requests in system = arrival rate × time in system. At 600/s in and 400/s out, the line grows by 200 every second.

Every request is eventually answered — after the client gave up — so capacity goes to answers nobody reads (low *goodput*). Fix: bound the queue by a latency budget (40 ÷ 400/s = 100 ms) and refuse the excess at once with `503` + `Retry-After`. Callers can also cap their in-flight requests (AIMD).

**Analogy:** a waiting list capped at what the kitchen serves in ten minutes; later guests hear "come back at eight".
Where the analogy breaks: clients that time out often *retry*, adding load exactly when it hurts.

```
open-model arrivals 600/s ─▶ Server.admit ─▶ [queue] ─▶ 4 workers ─▶ 400/s
                                 │ len(queue) >= MAX_QUEUE (build task)
                                 └─▶ 503, Retry-After = max(1, ceil(len/400))
caller AIMD: limit += 1/limit on 200, limit *= 0.9 on 503
```

The one thing to remember: bound every queue by a latency budget and shed the excess fast.

### Failure lab: a burst with an unbounded queue
1. **Inject:** `BURST` — 10 s at 150% of capacity between two 60% phases (seeded).
2. **Symptom:** step 2 — 0 errors, p99 4619.1 ms, goodput 3781 of 10686.
3. **Diagnose:** `Server.admit` accepts everything; Little's law says the wait grows by 200 ÷ 400 = 0.5 s for every second of overload.
4. **Fix (build task):** refuse when the queue holds `MAX_QUEUE = 40`, with `Retry-After` of at least 1 whole second.
5. **Proof** — **What you see** (`python solution_backpressure.py`, then `BUILD_TARGET=mine` tests on the unchanged file):
   ```
   {"t": 12, "offered": 603, "ok": 400, "shed": 203, "p99_ms": 167}
   summary: {"offered": 10686, "ok": 8779, "shed": 1907, "throughput_per_s": 293, "mean_wait_ms": 44.2, "p50_ms": 27.1, "p99_ms": 154.5, "goodput": 8779}
   AssertionError: 4619.1 not less than 250
   FAILED (failures=3)
   ```
   The server serves its 400/s and sheds the rest; goodput more than doubles.

6. **Real clock:** `python serve_realtime.py --check` — **What you see:**
   ```
   1200 requests at 120/s for 10 s (capacity 80/s, queue bound 8): {'200': 765, '503': 435, 'p99_ms': 197, '503_without_retry_after': 0}
   ```
   **What it means:** timing-based, so it differs slightly from the README. k6 is Expected (not run here): not installed.

## Common confusions
**Q:** No errors means the system coped. — **A:** Step 2 had zero errors and 35% goodput (3781 of 10686).

**Q:** A bigger queue is safer. — **A:** tryIt 1: 400 slots, p99 1115.2 ms, lower goodput.

**Q:** Shedding loses business. — **A:** Shed requests get a fast, retryable answer; unshed, most would time out.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>40 ÷ 400/s = 100 ms maximum queueing delay — a latency budget.</details>
