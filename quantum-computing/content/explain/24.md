# Foundations II: quantum information basics — explained

## The big picture

This lesson asks what lesson 23's algebra allows and forbids for information. Four results explain behaviour from the labs: why you cannot "save a copy" of a qubit to debug it; how a Bell pair plus two classical bits moves a state (and why that is not faster-than-light messaging); why one oracle query was enough in "Bernstein–Vazirani as a pattern"; and why "Noise models: how Bell and GHZ results degrade" switched from state vectors to density matrices. All four are checked numerically by `labs/L500/24-quantum-information-basics/theorems.py`.

### T24.1 No-cloning theorem

**In plain words:** no circuit can take an unknown qubit and output two perfect copies of it.

**Why it is true (intuition):** unitaries preserve overlaps (T23.1). Two copies of a pair of states have overlap *squared*. A number equal to its own square is 0 or 1, so a cloner can only work on states that are identical or orthogonal; `|0⟩` and `|+⟩` are neither. Skeleton:
1. Assume `U(|ψ⟩|e⟩) = |ψ⟩|ψ⟩` for all `ψ`.
2. Take inner products of two such equations: left side gives `⟨ψ|φ⟩`, right side `⟨ψ|φ⟩²`.
3. So `x = x²`, `x ∈ {0, 1}`.
4. `⟨0|+⟩ = 1/√2` contradicts this.
5. Alternative by linearity: copying `|0⟩` and `|1⟩` forces `|+⟩` to become a Bell state, not `|+⟩|+⟩`.

**See it in numbers:**
```text
  CNOT copier clones |0> and |1> exactly (fidelity 1)
  theta = 1.5708: joint fidelity 0.5000 = (c^3 + s^3)^2 = 0.5000; copy alone 0.5000 = c^4 + s^4
  minimum fidelity 0.5000 at theta = pi/2 (|+> becomes a Bell state, not |+>|+>)
  500 random unitaries keep <0,e|+,e> = 0.7071; perfect clones would need <0|+>^2 = 0.5000: impossible
```
The CNOT "copier" is perfect on basis states and worst on `|+⟩` (θ = π/2). The last line is the proof in numbers: unitaries keep overlap 0.7071; perfect clones would need 0.5000.

**Where it shows up in practice:** "Entanglement with a Bell pair": CNOT on `|+⟩|0⟩` makes a Bell pair, not two copies.

### T24.2 Teleportation and superdense coding

**In plain words:** with a shared Bell pair, 2 classical bits move one qubit's state exactly; and 1 qubit sent carries 2 classical bits.

**Why it is true (intuition):** rewrite "unknown state ⊗ Bell pair" in the Bell basis of Alice's two qubits. It splits into four equal branches; in each, Bob already holds `|ψ⟩` up to a known Pauli. Alice's measurement says which branch; Bob undoes the Pauli. Skeleton:
1. Expand `|ψ⟩|Φ+⟩` in the computational basis.
2. Substitute the inverse Bell relations for Alice's pair and collect terms.
3. Read off four branches, each `½ × |Bell⟩ ⊗ (Pauli)|ψ⟩`.
4. Each outcome has probability 1/4, independent of `ψ`: the bits reveal nothing.
5. Bob's correction restores `|ψ⟩`; before it, his state is `I/2`.

**See it in numbers:**
```text
  200 random states x 4 outcomes: each outcome has probability 0.25 (within 1e-12) and Bob's corrected qubit has fidelity 1 (within 1e-12): True
  superdense coding, message ab -> Bob decodes (q0 q1): 00 -> 00, 01 -> 01, 10 -> 10, 11 -> 11 with probability 1
```

**Where it shows up in practice:** the Bell measurement (CX then H) from lesson 23 is Alice's step; "Portable circuits with OpenQASM" uses the same gates.

### T24.3 Deutsch-Jozsa correctness

**In plain words:** one query decides "constant or balanced" with certainty, by checking whether all signs agree.

**Why it is true (intuition):** after `H`, oracle, `H`, the amplitude of `0…0` is the *average* of `(−1)^{f(x)}`. Constant: all signs equal, average ±1. Balanced: half cancel, average 0. Skeleton:
1. `H^{⊗n}|0⟩` = uniform superposition.
2. Phase kickback writes `(−1)^{f(x)}` on each branch.
3. Final `H^{⊗n}`: amplitude of `0^n` is `2^{-n} Σ_x (−1)^{f(x)}`.
4. Constant → `P = 1`; balanced → `P = 0`.
5. Classical deterministic worst case: an adversary answers `2^{n−1}` queries consistently with both cases, so `2^{n−1} + 1`.

**See it in numbers:**
```text
  n = 3: both constant functions give P(000) = 1.0000, 1.0000
  all 70 balanced functions give P(000) = 0.0000 (max)
  a deterministic classical algorithm needs 2^(n-1) + 1 = 5 queries in the worst case
```
70 is the number of ways to choose which 4 of 8 inputs output 1. The honest caveat: a *randomised* classical algorithm with a few queries errs rarely, so the separation is a teaching example.

**Where it shows up in practice:** "Bernstein–Vazirani as a pattern" is the same circuit with a different oracle; `no_kickback` showed what happens without the `|−⟩` ancilla.

### T24.4 Density matrices, partial trace and no-signalling

**In plain words:** a density matrix describes what one party can know; for half of a Bell pair that is "a fair coin", and nothing the other party does locally changes it.

**Why it is true (intuition):** `ρ` averages over what you do not know. Tracing out a qubit averages over its labels; for a Bell pair the off-diagonal terms need *both* labels to match, so they vanish. Operations on A can be moved around the trace over A and cancel. Skeleton:
1. `ρ = Σ p_i|ψ_i⟩⟨ψ_i|` is Hermitian, PSD, trace 1; `Tr ρ² ≤ 1`, equality iff pure.
2. `Tr_A |Φ+⟩⟨Φ+| = I/2`, purity 1/2.
3. Cyclicity of the trace over A: `Tr_A[(U⊗I)ρ(U†⊗I)] = Tr_A ρ`.
4. The same for an unread measurement on A.

**See it in numbers:**
```text
  Bell pair: global purity Tr(rho^2) = 1.0000; rho_A = rho_B = I/2: True; Tr(rho_B^2) = 0.5000
  no signalling: Alice's random unitary or unread measurement changes rho_B by < 1e-12: True
  mixture 1/2|0><0| + 1/2|+><+|: eigenvalues [0.8536, 0.1464], purity 0.7500
```
The whole is pure (1.0000) while each half is maximally mixed (0.5000): entanglement in two numbers.

**Where it shows up in practice:** "Noise models: how Bell and GHZ results degrade" (`dm.run`, fidelity from `ρ`) and "Error mitigation: readout correction and zero-noise extrapolation".

## Worked problems

**P5.** Deutsch–Jozsa, `n = 2`, `f(x1, x2) = x1 XOR x2`. Find `P(00)` and the classical worst case for `n = 10`.
1. Tabulate `f`: 00→0, 01→1, 10→1, 11→0. *Why:* the amplitude formula needs every sign.
2. Signs `(−1)^f`: +1, −1, −1, +1. *Why:* phase kickback turns outputs into signs.
3. `a_0 = (1 − 1 − 1 + 1)/4 = 0`, so `P(00) = 0`: "balanced". *Why:* T24.3 step 3.
4. Classical: `2^9 + 1 = 513` queries. *Why:* the adversary argument with `n = 10`.

**P6.** `|ψ⟩ = √0.8|00⟩ + √0.2|11⟩`. Find `ρ_A`, its purity and entropy.
1. Write `|ψ⟩⟨ψ|`: terms `0.8|00⟩⟨00|`, `0.2|11⟩⟨11|`, and two cross terms `√0.16 |00⟩⟨11|` etc. *Why:* outer product of the expansion.
2. Trace out B: keep terms whose B-labels match; cross terms have B-labels 0 vs 1 and vanish. `ρ_A = diag(0.8, 0.2)`. *Why:* the partial trace sums over B's diagonal.
3. Purity `0.8² + 0.2² = 0.68`. *Why:* `Tr ρ² = Σ λ²`.
4. Entropy `−(0.8 log₂ 0.8 + 0.2 log₂ 0.2) = 0.7219` bits. *Why:* less than a Bell pair's 1 bit: partly entangled.

## Common confusions

- **Q:** CNOT copies qubits. — **A:** Only basis states; on `|+⟩` fidelity is 0.5000.
- **Q:** Teleportation is faster than light. — **A:** Each outcome is 0.25 regardless of `ψ`; Bob has `I/2` until the bits arrive.
- **Q:** A mixed state is a superposition. — **A:** The mixture's purity is 0.7500; a superposition is pure (1.0000).
