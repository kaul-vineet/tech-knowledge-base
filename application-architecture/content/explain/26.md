# Ethics and professional responsibility: accessibility, privacy by design and honest technical debt — explained

## The big picture
Every earlier lesson turned a quality into something measurable and gated: tenant isolation in "Multi-tenancy: shared schema vs schema per tenant vs database per tenant", secrets that never print in "Configuration the 12-factor way", masked decision logs in "Policy as code: RBAC vs ABAC, policy tests and decision logs", honest numbers checked by code in every agent assist. This lesson asks what happens when the pressure is not technical but social: a deadline, a stakeholder who wants a reassuring sentence, and a team that wants more data. The architect's job does not change — state facts, name trade-offs, make the decision traceable — but now the people bearing the cost may never be in the room. Lesson 24's utility tree already makes the point in code: `python check_quality.py` prints, among its highest-priority scenarios, `(H,M) interaction capability: A screen-reader user completes 'borrow a book' with keyboard only; WCA...`. Accessibility is a quality attribute, not a polish task.

## The case in one paragraph
Online borrowing launches in two weeks. An audit finds three WCAG 2.2 AA failures in the borrow flow: a distorted-text sign-in puzzle (SC 3.3.8), drag-only reordering (SC 2.5.7) and tiny renew buttons (SC 2.5.8). The fix takes about three weeks because sign-in is a third-party component. Analytics wants full loan histories kept forever, on by default. The product owner wants you to tell the city council the service is "WCAG 2.2 AA compliant and privacy by design" and to file the rest as "minor tech debt".

## Worked analysis with the ACM Code of Ethics (2018), step by step
**Step 1 — separate facts from assumptions.** *Facts:* three AA failures in the core flow; about three weeks to fix; a request for indefinite, default-on history. *Assumptions:* which accessibility laws bind this city (public-sector rules may, the European Accessibility Act may not) — that is a question for the DPO and legal, not for the architect to guess. *Why this step:* principle 2.5 asks for thorough evaluation including risks; you cannot evaluate what you have not separated.

**Step 2 — list who bears the consequences.** Blind, low-vision, motor- and cognitively-impaired members; all members whose reading history would be stored; library staff handling fallbacks; the council (legally responsible); the delivery team; the analytics team. *Why:* principle 1.2 (avoid harm) is about *who* is harmed, and the people harmed by the puzzle are absent from the meeting.

**Step 3 — test the requested statement against 1.3 (honest and trustworthy).** "WCAG 2.2 AA compliant" is false while three AA criteria fail. "Privacy by design" is false while history is kept indefinitely by default — GDPR Art. 25(2) asks for data protection *by default*, meaning only necessary data and limited storage. A false claim to a funder is not a gray area. *Why:* the Code forbids deliberately false or misleading claims; the honest alternative must be specific enough to be useful.

**Step 4 — apply 1.4 (fair, non-discriminatory) and 1.6 (privacy) to the options.**
- (A) Launch and claim compliance: excludes disabled members from a public service *and* is dishonest. Rejected.
- (B) Delay three weeks: honest and inclusive; costs the deadline.
- (C) Launch on time with an equivalent accessible path (alternative sign-in that meets 3.3.8, keyboard "move up/down" controls, larger targets via hotfix), a truthful conformance statement listing gaps, dates and owners, and (B) as fallback if the path is not genuinely equivalent.
For history: keep active loans only; delete or anonymise returned loans after N days; recommendations become opt-in, revocable and purpose-limited, fed by pseudonymised events.

**Step 5 — apply 3.7 (systems in society's infrastructure) and decide.** A public library is infrastructure; libraries have long treated circulation records as confidential. Recommend (C), with (B) as the fallback, and the privacy-by-default design.

**Step 6 — make it stick.** Put accessibility and retention into the utility tree and release gates: an automated accessibility check plus a manual screen-reader pass per release; a retention fitness function that fails if any returned loan older than N days is identifiable. Record the decision in an ADR with the debt's owner and due date. *Why:* principle 2.5 again — an evaluation that is not re-run decays.

## A contrasting view: consequences first
A strictly consequentialist reading starts from totals. Launching on time serves thousands of members two weeks earlier; three weeks of delay harms all of them a little to protect some of them a lot; analytics data may improve recommendations for everyone. On this view (A) could even win if the harm to excluded members is "small and temporary".

Where it agrees with the ACM analysis: it also rejects the *false statement*, because discovered dishonesty destroys trust in the whole service — a large future cost — and enforcement precedents (Robles v. Domino's on web accessibility under the ADA; the Irish DPC's €265 million fine against Meta under GDPR Art. 25) make legal costs concrete. Where it disagrees: it is willing to trade exclusion against convenience if the numbers favour it. The duty-based reading says exclusion from a public service is not a currency to be traded, and that people who carry the cost must be told. In practice both views converge on option (C) — they differ on whether (B) is required if (C)'s alternative path turns out to be inferior.

## Worked problems
**P26-2 — rewrite the statement honestly.**
1. Name the standard and level: WCAG 2.2 AA. *Why:* vague "accessible" claims cannot be checked.
2. Name the gaps: sign-in puzzle, drag-only reordering, small renew buttons.
3. Name the mitigation and dates: an accessible alternative from day one, full conformance by a stated date, with an owner.
4. State the privacy default: history deleted N days after return unless the member opts in. *Why this works:* every sentence is true and checkable (ACM 1.3, 2.5), and the council still gets a launch.

**P26-5 — when is technical debt ethical?**
1. Condition one: those who pay the interest know and agree — the debt is disclosed, owned and dated.
2. Condition two: the interest does not fall on people who cannot consent and are seriously harmed.
3. Counter-example: calling an inaccessible sign-in "minor tech debt" shifts the interest onto disabled members who were never asked. *Why:* Cunningham's metaphor assumed the borrower pays.

## Common confusions
**Q:** An accessible alternative path makes the service compliant. — **A:** It mitigates harm; the honest statement still lists the failing criteria until they pass.

**Q:** Privacy by design means encrypting the data. — **A:** Art. 25(2) is about *defaults*: collect and keep only what is necessary.

**Q:** Ethics is the product owner's call, not the architect's. — **A:** The ACM Code binds the professional who signs the evaluation; principle 2.5 is about you.
