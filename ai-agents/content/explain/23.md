### T1 Softmax with temperature is the maximum-entropy (Gibbs) distribution
**In plain words.** Of all ways to spread probability over tokens, softmax at temperature T is the one that best balances "favour high scores" against "stay uncertain"; T is the exchange rate between the two.

**Why it is true (intuition).** You want high expected score but also a spread-out distribution. Push all mass on the best token and you lose entropy; spread evenly and you lose score. The best compromise gives each token mass proportional to `exp(score / T)`.
1. Write the objective F(p) = Σ pᵢzᵢ + T·H(p) with the constraint Σ pᵢ = 1.
2. Add a Lagrange multiplier and set each derivative to zero: zᵢ − T(log pᵢ + 1) + λ = 0.
3. Solve: pᵢ ∝ exp(zᵢ/T); normalising gives softmax.
4. F is strictly concave and the entropy term pushes away from the boundary, so this point is the unique maximum, with value T·log Σ exp(zⱼ/T).
5. Differentiating entropy with β = 1/T gives dH/dT = Var(z)/T³ ≥ 0: higher temperature never lowers entropy.

**See it in numbers.** `python tiny_lm.py check T1`:
```
Gibbs objective E[z]+T*H at T=0.7: softmax 2.2211; best of 10000 random distributions 2.2168; T*log(sum exp(z/T)) = 2.2211
T=0.05: max prob 1.0000 at index 0 (argmax of z = 0)
T=100 : max |p - 1/5| = 0.0057
entropy over T=0.1..10 strictly increasing: True (H from 0.0005 to 1.5953; log 5 = 1.6094)
dH/dT at T=1.3: finite difference 0.396496; Var(z)/T^3 = 0.396496
```
Softmax reaches 2.2211, equal to the closed form; 10,000 random distributions all fall short (best 2.2168). At T = 0.05 it is greedy; at T = 100 it is within 0.0057 of uniform over 5 tokens. Entropy climbs towards log 5 = 1.6094, and the derivative matches Var(z)/T³ to six decimals.

**Where it shows up.** "Call a model over HTTP": `temperature` picks a point on this trade-off; `0` is the greedy limit.

### T2 Why attention divides by sqrt(d)
**In plain words.** A dot product of two random d-dimensional vectors grows like √d; dividing by √d keeps attention scores the same size at every model width.

**Why it is true.** Summing d independent zero-mean products adds their variances.
1. Each qᵢkᵢ has mean 0 and variance 1 (independent, unit variance).
2. Variances of independent terms add: Var(q·k) = d.
3. Dividing by √d gives variance 1.
4. Unscaled, scores of spread √d make softmax nearly one-hot; its Jacobian diag(p) − ppᵀ → 0, so gradients vanish.

**See it in numbers.** `check T2`:
```
d=512: mean(q.k)/sqrt(d) = -0.015  var(q.k)/d = 1.000  var(q.k/sqrt(d)) = 1.000
d=512, 16 keys, unscaled: mean max weight 0.9439  mean entropy 0.1366 (log 16 = 2.7726)  mean ||Jacobian|| 0.0740
d=512, 16 keys, scaled  : mean max weight 0.2461  mean entropy 2.3576 (log 16 = 2.7726)  mean ||Jacobian|| 0.3053
```
Unscaled, one key takes 94% of the weight and the gradient norm is about 4× smaller than scaled.

**Where it shows up.** The context window in "Multi-agent patterns: when one agent with tools is enough" is the set of keys every query attends over.

### T3 Gibbs' inequality: counting minimises cross-entropy; perplexity = exp(cross-entropy)
**In plain words.** The model that best predicts its training text is the one that copies its frequencies; perplexity is "how many equally likely choices per token" the model feels.

**Why it is true.** log is concave, so averaging under p of log(q/p) is at most log of the average, which is log 1 = 0.
1. H(p, q) − H(p) = −Σ p log(q/p).
2. Jensen: Σ p log(q/p) ≤ log Σ q ≤ 0.
3. Equality only when q = p, so counting (MLE) wins on the training data.
4. Perplexity = exp(H): same minimiser; uniform over V gives V; one zero-probability test bigram gives ∞.

**See it in numbers.** `check T3`:
```
MLE training cross-entropy 0.7233 nats/token, perplexity 2.0612
1000 perturbed models: 1000 have higher cross-entropy; lowest 0.7313
uniform model perplexity 11.0000 (= V)
held-out perplexity: MLE (k=0) inf; add-0.1 4.4475; add-1 6.7417
```
All 1,000 perturbations are worse; on held-out text the unsmoothed MLE is infinite — smoothing trades training fit for robustness.

**Where it shows up.** "Evaluating agents with a golden set": a model's fit on training text says nothing about unseen cases.

### T4 Truncated sampling is sampling conditioned on the kept set
**In plain words.** Top-k and top-p just delete unlikely tokens and renormalise the rest.

**Why it is true.** Restricting to S and dividing by p(S) is the definition of conditioning on W ∈ S. Greedy is top-k with k = 1, and the T → 0 limit of T1.

**See it in numbers.** `check T4`:
```
{'method': 'top_p', 'p': 0.6}: keeps ['agent', 'tool'] (mass 0.625); max |empirical - p/mass| = 0.0058
greedy == top_k(1): True; softmax(log p / 0.01) max |diff| to greedy = 0.0000
```

**Where it shows up.** "Structured output": temperature 0 (greedy) for JSON because you want the single most likely format, not variety.

## Worked problems
**l23-p2: softmax of z = (2, 1, 0) at T = 1 and T = 0.5, with entropies.**
1. *Exponentiate at T = 1* (why: softmax is exp then normalise): 7.389, 2.718, 1; sum 11.107.
2. *Normalise:* p = (0.6652, 0.2447, 0.0900).
3. *At T = 0.5 divide logits by T first* (why: temperature scales logits): (4, 2, 0) → p = (0.8668, 0.1173, 0.0159).
4. *Entropy −Σ p ln p* (why: it measures spread): 0.8324 vs 0.4411 nats.
5. *Interpret:* lower T is sharper; entropy fell by 0.3913 nats, as T1's dH/dT ≥ 0 predicts. My run of `tiny_lm.softmax` printed exactly these values.

**l23-p4: probability and perplexity of "the agent calls the tool ." after "the".**
1. *Read the bigram counts* (why: MLE = count ratio, T3): P(agent|the) = 3/8, P(calls|agent) = 1/3, P(the|calls) = 1/1, P(tool|the) = 2/8, P(.|tool) = 1/2 — counted from `tiny_lm.CORPUS` here.
2. *Multiply* (why: chain rule over 5 predicted tokens): 0.015625.
3. *Perplexity* = 0.015625^(−1/5) = 2.2974 (why: geometric-mean inverse probability per token).
4. *Interpret:* about 2.3 effective choices per token, far below the uniform model's 11.

## Common confusions
**Q:** Temperature 0 makes the model "more accurate". — **A:** It makes it greedy (T1's limit); greedy can loop, e.g. "the agent calls the agent calls…" in this lab.

**Q:** Low training perplexity means a good model. — **A:** T3's MLE has training perplexity 2.0612 and held-out perplexity inf.

**Q:** √d scaling is a tuning trick. — **A:** It follows from Var(q·k) = d; the check shows variance 1.000 after scaling at d = 512.
