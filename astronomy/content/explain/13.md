# Calibration frames: bias, dark, flat and cosmic-ray rejection — explained

## The intuition
A raw CCD frame is not the sky. Each pixel adds an electronic offset (bias, ~1000 ADU) and thermal signal growing with exposure (dark), and multiplies the light by its own sensitivity, including vignetting and dust (flat). Calibration subtracts bias and dark, divides by a normalised flat, then repairs cosmic rays.

Order matters: if the bias is still inside the flat when you normalise it, the pedestal flattens the response map and division under-corrects, so a star drifting across the chip appears to vary.

**Analogy:** calibrating a kitchen scale: subtract the empty-tray reading before you measure the scale's gain, or the gain comes out wrong.
Where the analogy breaks: the error appears only when the star moves across pixels of different sensitivity.

```
bias frames --sigma_clip_mean--> master bias
dark frames --sigma_clip_mean - bias--> master dark
flat frames --sigma_clip_mean (- bias!)--> / median --> master flat
light: (raw - bias - dark) / flat --clean_cosmics()--> photometry() --> flux per frame
```

The one thing to remember: a flat must contain light only; remove the bias before normalising.

## Walkthrough: what actually happens when you run it
### Step 1 — the shipped reduction
**You run** `python calibrate.py`

**What happens inside** `camera.frames(13)` simulates calibration frames and 30 light frames of a constant star drifting from x = 12 to 52 through vignetting and a dust donut. `masters()` combines pixel by pixel with 3-sigma MAD clipping.

**What you see**
```
{"rms_percent": 0.88, "robust_rms_percent": 0.899, "expected_noise_percent": 0.224, "variable": true}
master_flat min/max: 0.8527 1.0792 (true response 0.75/1.00)
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** A constant star is declared variable: its scatter is four times the photon floor.

### Step 2 — read the log
**You run** `head -3 calib_log.jsonl`

**What happens inside** `run()` writes one JSON line per step and per frame.

**What you see**
```
{"step": "master_bias", "median_adu": 999.95}
{"step": "master_dark", "median_adu": 2.04, "max_adu": 205.1}
{"step": "master_flat", "min": 0.8527, "max": 1.0792}
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** Bias near 1000 and dark near 2.0 ADU (0.05 e⁻/s × 60 s / 1.5 e⁻/ADU) are right; max 205.1 is hot pixels. A dark median near 0 would mean the bias was subtracted twice.

### Failure lab 1/4 — inject and symptom
**You run** the shipped `masters()`: `flat = sigma_clip_mean(f["flat"])` with no bias subtraction.

**What happens inside** Each flat pixel is 1000 + response × light. Dividing by the median shrinks the contrast: the darkest corner reads 0.8527 instead of about 0.821.

**What you see**
```
relative flux, every 5th frame: 0.9971 1.0070 1.0082 1.0031 1.0043 0.9881
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** The light curve wanders by about 1 % as the star crosses regions the flat under-corrects. Cosmic-ray cleaning is not the culprit: the log shows 3 hits cleaned per frame, 4 on frame 17.

### Failure lab 2/4 — diagnose
**You run** `BUILD_TARGET=mine python -m unittest test_build_task`

**What happens inside** Tests check the flat and seeds 20–29.

**What you see**
```
AssertionError: 0.05086357058515789 not less than 0.03
FAILED (failures=12)
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** The flat is off by 5 % somewhere, and every night is "variable": a systematic error, not bad luck.

### Failure lab 3/4 — fix
**You run** `python solution/calibrate.py`

**What happens inside** One line changes: `sigma_clip_mean(f["flat"] - bias)` before normalising.

**What you see**
```
{"rms_percent": 0.389, "robust_rms_percent": 0.382, "expected_noise_percent": 0.224, "variable": false}
master_flat min/max: 0.813 1.1009 (true response 0.75/1.00)
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** The star is constant again. A flat minimum still near 0.85 would mean the bias was subtracted after normalising.

### Failure lab 4/4 — proof
**You run** `python -m unittest test_build_task -v`, then `python try_it.py`.

**What happens inside** The solution passes the same tests; try_it reruns the shipped flat with 30 000 e⁻ flats.

**What you see**
```
Ran 3 tests in 6.565s
OK
2. shipped flat, 30k e- flats: robust rms 0.366%, variable=False, flat min 0.8267 (fixed pipeline gives 0.813 at 6k e-)
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** Brighter flats make the bias a smaller fraction and hide the bug — which is why it survives in real pipelines.

## Common confusions
- **Q:** "The dark already includes the bias, so subtract only the dark." — **A:** `masters()` stores dark − bias, so both are subtracted.
- **Q:** "Cosmic-ray cleaning will also eat star peaks." — **A:** `clean_cosmics()` needs 3× its neighbours; a PSF peak is ~1.4×.
- **Q:** "Plain rms is enough." — **A:** try_it 1 shows one uncleaned hit gives rms 2.96 % but robust rms 0.377 %.
