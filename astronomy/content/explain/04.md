# Units and scales: AU, light-year, parsec, magnitudes and distance modulus — explained

## The intuition
Units keep huge distances readable. The AU is exact by definition; the light-year is c times a Julian year; the parsec is the distance at which 1 AU subtends one arcsecond.

Brightness uses magnitudes: logarithmic and backwards. 5 magnitudes is a flux factor of 100; smaller is brighter. Flux falls as 1/d², so every ×10 in distance adds 5 magnitudes: the distance modulus m − M.

**Analogy:** decibels — a log scale turns multiplying into adding.
Where the analogy breaks: magnitudes run backwards, and 5 steps = ×100.

```
AU (exact) --x 648000/pi--> pc        c x 365.25 d --> ly
flux ratio <--10^(-0.4 dm)--> magnitude difference
d [pc] --5 log10(d/10)--> m - M ;  m = M + (m - M)
```

The one thing to remember: m − M = 5 log10(d / 10 pc).

## Walkthrough: what actually happens when you run it
### Step 1 — distances
**You run** `python units.py`

**What happens inside** Constants: `PC = AU * 648_000.0 / math.pi`, `LY = C * JULIAN_YEAR`.

**What you see**
```
1 AU = 149597870700 m | 1 ly = 9460730472580800 m | 1 pc = 3.085678e+16 m
1 pc = 206264.806 AU = 3.2616 ly | light from 1 AU takes 499.005 s
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** 206264.806 is arcseconds per radian; another light-year means 365-day years.

### Step 2 — magnitudes
**You run** the same command (lines 3–4).

**What happens inside** `flux_ratio()` is `10 ** (-0.4 * (m1 - m2))`; `mag_difference()` inverts it.

**What you see**
```
5 mag brighter = x100.0 flux | 1 mag = x2.5119
flux x2 -> -0.7526 mag
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** Doubling flux lowers the magnitude; a positive sign means a missing minus.

### Step 3 — distance modulus
**You run** the same command (last lines).

**What happens inside** `distance_modulus(d)` is added to M = 4.83 (the Sun).

**What you see**
```
star with M = 4.83 at 1000 pc: m =  14.83 (distance modulus 10.00)
same star at 1 AU (the Sun): m = -26.74
parallax 0.1 arcsec -> 10.0 pc; modulus 5.00 -> 100.0 pc
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** −26.74 for the Sun is the sanity check; ln breaks it. Tests: `Ran 8 tests ... OK (skipped=1)`.

## Common confusions
- **Q:** "A light-year is a time." — **A:** it is 9460730472580800 m.
- **Q:** "Magnitude 10 is twice as bright as 5." — **A:** it is 100 times fainter.
- **Q:** "M changes with distance." — **A:** M is the fixed 10 pc value.
