### Hubble's law from homogeneous expansion, and the low-z redshift-distance relation
**In plain words** If space stretches uniformly, every observer sees every galaxy receding at a speed proportional to its distance — no one is at the centre. At small redshift, cz = H0 d; slightly farther, a correction of about 0.76 z appears.

**Why it is true (intuition)** Uniform stretching multiplies every separation by the same factor, so the rate of change of a separation is proportional to the separation itself, from wherever you measure.
1. Positions scale as rᵢ = a(t) xᵢ with fixed comoving xᵢ.
2. Any separation rᵢⱼ = a (xⱼ − xᵢ); differentiate: ṙᵢⱼ = (ȧ/a) rᵢⱼ.
3. Index i is arbitrary, so the law holds for every observer.
4. Light's wavelength stretches with a, so 1 + z = 1/a(t_emit); to first order z ≈ H0 d/c.

**See it in numbers**
```
observer at galaxy 137: v/r ranges 67.4000000000 .. 67.4000000000 km/s/Mpc, min cos(angle v, r) = 1.000000000000
D_L / (cz/H0) - 1 at z = [0.001, 0.002, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02]: ['0.00076', '0.00153', '0.00381', '0.00761', '0.01516']
small-z slope 0.7620 vs (1 - q0)/2 = 0.7638 with q0 = Om/2 - OL = -0.5275
Hubble time 1/H0: H0 = 67.4 -> 14.51 Gyr; Hubble (1929) K = 500 -> 1.96 Gyr
```
From galaxy 137, every v/r is 67.4 and every velocity points straight away (cos = 1). The luminosity-distance correction grows linearly with slope 0.7620, matching (1 − q0)/2. 1/H0 sets the time scale: 14.51 Gyr today, 1.96 Gyr with Hubble's 1929 value.

**Where it shows up in practice** Lesson 24's rung 3 stayed below z = 0.05; lesson 27 re-checks Hubble's 1.96 Gyr.

### Critical density and the age of a flat matter + Lambda universe
**In plain words** Flat means density exactly 3H²/(8πG); with matter and Λ the age has a closed form in H0 and Ω_m.

**Why it is true (intuition)** A shell of galaxies around any point behaves like a ball thrown upward: its energy decides whether it escapes; flat means "exactly escape speed".
1. A test mass on a uniform expanding sphere conserves energy: ½ṙ² − GM/r = const.
2. Divide by ½r²: H² = (8πG/3)ρ + const/a² — the Friedmann equation.
3. Flat (const = 0) means ρ = 3H²/(8πG).
4. Matter dilutes as a⁻³, Λ does not: H = H0 √(Ω_m a⁻³ + Ω_Λ).
5. dt = da/(aH); integrate from a = 0 to 1 (substitute u = a^(3/2)) to get the asinh closed form.

**See it in numbers**
```
critical density 3H0^2/(8 pi G) = 8.5331e-27 kg/m^3 = 1.2608e+11 Msun/Mpc^3
age, flat LCDM (Om = 0.315): integral 13.7962 Gyr, closed form 13.7962 Gyr, RK4 Friedmann ODE 13.7962 Gyr
Einstein-de Sitter (Om = 1): 2/(3 H0) = 9.67 Gyr
```
About five hydrogen atoms per cubic metre; three independent routes give 13.7962 Gyr. Without Λ the age would be 9.67 Gyr — younger than the oldest stars.

**Where it shows up in practice** Problem 25.4: why Λ was needed.

### Bayes' theorem and detailed balance for Metropolis-Hastings
**In plain words** A posterior is likelihood × prior, normalised. Metropolis–Hastings draws from it without ever computing the normalisation, because every move is balanced by its reverse.

**Why it is true (intuition)** In equilibrium, the flow from x to y must equal the flow back. The acceptance rule is built so that it does, pair by pair.
1. Product rule: p(θ, D) = p(D|θ)p(θ) = p(θ|D)p(D); divide by p(D).
2. For x ≠ y, flow π(x)K(x, y) = min(π(x)q(y|x), π(y)q(x|y)) — symmetric in x and y.
3. Symmetric flow is detailed balance; summing over x gives πK = π.
4. Only π(y)/π(x) enters, so p(D) cancels.
5. Without the Hastings factor, an asymmetric proposal breaks the symmetry.

**See it in numbers**
```
symmetric proposal: max |pi_i K_ij - pi_j K_ji| = 0.0e+00; max |pi K - pi| = 5.6e-17
200000-step chain: max |frequency - pi| = 0.0027
biased proposal 0.8/0.2: total-variation distance to pi without Hastings factor 0.175, with it 2.7e-14
```
The exact transition matrix balances to machine precision; a long chain's visit frequencies match π to 0.0027; a biased proposal without the Hastings factor converges to the wrong distribution (0.175 away).

**Where it shows up in practice** Lesson 15's `mcmc.py` uses a symmetric Gaussian proposal, so its Hastings factor is 1.

### Closed-form posterior for H0 (Gaussian likelihood, Gaussian or flat prior)
**In plain words** For v = H0 d + Gaussian noise, the posterior for H0 is Gaussian: a precision-weighted average of the data's slope and the prior mean.

**Why it is true (intuition)** The log-posterior is a sum of squares in H0; completing the square leaves one Gaussian.
1. ln p = −Σ(vᵢ − H0 dᵢ)²/2σ² − (H0 − μ0)²/2τ² + const.
2. Collect H0² terms: precision 1/s² = Σdᵢ²/σ² + 1/τ².
3. Collect H0 terms: mean m = s²(Σvᵢdᵢ/σ² + μ0/τ²).
4. Flat prior (τ → ∞): m is the least-squares slope.

**See it in numbers**
```
20 synthetic galaxies, sigma_v = 300 km/s, true H0 = 70: closed form 71.696 +/- 2.116
grid posterior 71.696 +/- 2.116; Metropolis (55000 kept, acceptance 0.61) 71.688 +/- 2.098
with Gaussian prior N(67.4, 0.5): posterior 67.627 +/- 0.487
with Gaussian prior N(73.0, 1.0): posterior 72.762 +/- 0.904
```
Formula, grid and sampler agree. Tight priors centred on the two sides of the "Hubble tension" pull the same 20 galaxies to 67.627 or 72.762.

**Where it shows up in practice** Lesson 15's fits: a flat prior is least squares.

## Worked problems
**Problem 25.7 — base rates in an alert stream.** *Why Bayes:* we know P(flag | real) = 0.9 and P(flag | bogus) = 0.05 but want P(real | flag). *Numerator:* 0.9 × 0.01 = 0.009. *Denominator* (all flagged): 0.009 + 0.05 × 0.99 = 0.0585. *Answer:* 0.009/0.0585 = 0.1538. With 50 % real: 0.45/(0.45 + 0.025) = 0.9474. *Why it matters:* lesson 18's broker cuts raise the prior first.

**Problem 25.8 — three galaxies, two priors.** *Sums first*, because the closed form needs only them: Σvd = 750·10 + 1350·20 + 2900·40 = 150500; Σd² = 2100. *Flat prior:* m = 150500/2100 = 71.667; s = 300/√2100 = 6.547. *Prior N(67.4, 0.5):* precision 2100/300² + 1/0.25 = 4.0233; mean (150500/90000 + 67.4/0.25)/4.0233 = 67.425; s = 0.499. *Why this result:* the prior carries far more information than three galaxies, so the posterior is the prior — always report it.

## Common confusions
- **Q:** "Galaxies fly apart from a centre." — **A:** galaxy 137 sees the same 67.4 as galaxy 0.
- **Q:** "MCMC needs the normalised posterior." — **A:** only ratios enter; p(D) cancels.
- **Q:** "A posterior is objective if the data are." — **A:** priors N(67.4, 0.5) and N(73.0, 1.0) give 67.627 and 72.762 from identical data.
