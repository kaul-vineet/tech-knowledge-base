# Light and spectra: blackbody, Wien's law, Doppler shift and redshift — explained

## The intuition
Starlight carries two messages. The spectrum's overall shape gives temperature: hotter bodies peak at shorter wavelengths (Wien) and emit much more in total (σT⁴). Narrow lines give motion: shifted to longer wavelength means moving away. The lab computes each quantity twice (law and numerical Planck curve) and compares.

**Analogy:** a stove ring glowing from red to white as it heats, and a siren dropping in pitch as it drives away.
Where the analogy breaks: light needs no medium, and at high speed you need the relativistic formula.

```
T --planck(lambda, T)--> spectrum --argmax--> peak  (vs b/T)
spectrum --pi x trapezoid--> total flux        (vs sigma T^4)
observed / rest line --> z --> cz or relativistic v
```

The one thing to remember: spectral shape gives temperature; line shifts give velocity.

## Walkthrough: what actually happens when you run it
### Step 1 — Wien peaks
**You run** `python light.py`

**What happens inside** `peak_wavelength()` evaluates `planck()` (with `expm1`) on 200 001 log-spaced wavelengths and takes the argmax.

**What you see**
```
Sun              T=  5772 K  Wien b/T =   502.0 nm  numerical peak =   502.0 nm
Rigel-like       T= 12100 K  Wien b/T =   239.5 nm  numerical peak =   239.5 nm
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** Both agree; B per frequency peaks elsewhere (a classic bug).

### Step 2 — total flux
**You run** the same command (line 4).

**What happens inside** `total_flux()` integrates Planck from 10 nm to 1 cm with `np.trapezoid`, times π.

**What you see**
```
Sun: pi * integral B = 6.2939e+07 W/m^2 vs sigma T^4 = 6.2939e+07 W/m^2
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** A grid too narrow loses the infrared tail and comes out low.

### Step 3 — Doppler and redshift
**You run** the same command (last lines).

**What happens inside** `doppler_velocity()` uses c·Δλ/λ; `relativistic_velocity(z)` uses ((1+z)²−1)/((1+z)²+1).

**What you see**
```
H-alpha observed at 656.50 nm: v = +100.5 km/s (z = 0.000335)
z = 0.1  : cz =     29979 km/s  relativistic Doppler v =     28487 km/s
z = 1.0  : cz =    299792 km/s  relativistic Doppler v =    179875 km/s
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** +0.22 nm is +100.5 km/s, receding. At z = 1, cz equals c, which no galaxy can recede at. Tests: `Ran 8 tests ... OK (skipped=1)`.

## Common confusions
- **Q:** "The Sun peaks at 502 nm, so it is green." — **A:** the eye sums the broad curve to white.
- **Q:** "z = 1 means light speed." — **A:** relativistic v = 179875 km/s.
- **Q:** "Positive velocity means approaching." — **A:** positive = receding.
