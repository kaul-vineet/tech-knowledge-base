# Foundations II: stars and radiation - luminosity, the HR diagram and the distance ladder — explained

## The big picture
Lesson 6 ("Light and spectra: blackbody, Wien's law, Doppler shift and redshift") checked numerically that π∫B dλ equals σT⁴; lesson 4 ("Units and scales: AU, light-year, parsec, magnitudes and distance modulus") used d = 1/p and m − M = 5 log10(d/10). This lesson proves both, adds the mass–luminosity–lifetime scaling, and follows a distance error up the ladder to H0. The ladder in the lab is synthetic and seeded: its Cepheid period–luminosity constants (M_V = −2.43 (log P − 1) − 4.05) and the supernova M = −19.3 are illustrative round numbers, not a published calibration. Only the ℓ Car parallax is real (Benedict et al. 2007).

All numbers below come from `python stars.py` (labs/L500/24-foundations-stars).

### Stefan-Boltzmann law from Planck's law, and L = 4 pi R^2 sigma T^4
**In plain words** A blackbody emits σT⁴ per square metre, σ built from h, c and k; a sphere emits L = 4πR²σT⁴, so equal-radius stars lie on slope-4 lines of the HR diagram.

**Why it is true (intuition)** Integrate Planck's curve over all frequencies; after a change of variable all the temperature dependence pops out as T⁴, leaving one pure number.
1. The flux through a surface from isotropic radiation is π B_ν (the cos θ integral over a hemisphere).
2. Substitute x = hν/kT: the integral becomes (2πk⁴T⁴/c²h³) ∫ x³/(eˣ − 1) dx.
3. Expand 1/(eˣ − 1) as Σ e^(−nx); each term integrates to 6/n⁴.
4. Σ 6/n⁴ = 6ζ(4) = π⁴/15, giving σ = 2π⁵k⁴/(15c²h³).
5. Multiply by the area 4πR²; take logs: log L = 2 log R + 4 log T + const.

**See it in numbers**
```
int_0^inf x^3/(e^x - 1) dx: quadrature 6.4939394023, series 6*sum(1/n^4) 6.4939391504, pi^4/15 6.4939394023
sigma = 2 pi^5 k^4 / (15 c^2 h^3) = 5.670374419e-08 W m^-2 K^-4 (CODATA 5.670374419e-08)
4 pi R_sun^2 sigma T_sun^4 = 3.8280e+26 W vs nominal L_sun 3.8280e+26 W (rel. diff 2.4e-06)
HR diagram: at fixed R, d log L / d log T = 4.000000, 4.000000 (lines of constant radius have slope 4)
```
Three routes agree (the truncated series is slightly low, as a partial sum must be); σ from exact constants equals CODATA; the nominal Sun fits to 2.4e-06.

**Where it shows up in practice** Lesson 6's `total_flux()` printed 6.2939e+07 W/m² for 5772 K — the same number this theorem predicts.

### Mass-luminosity scaling and main-sequence lifetime (homology sketch)
**In plain words** Heavier main-sequence stars are much brighter, roughly L ∝ M³ to M⁴, so they burn out much faster: t ∝ M/L ∝ M^(−2.5) for L ∝ M^3.5.

**Why it is true (intuition)** Replace each structure equation by its typical size: pressure holds up the weight, the gas law gives temperature, radiation leaks out through the opacity. The radius cancels.
1. Hydrostatic equilibrium: P ∼ GM²/R⁴.
2. Ideal gas: T ∼ μm_H GM/(kR).
3. Radiative diffusion: L ∼ acR⁴T⁴/(κM).
4. Substitute T: L ∼ μ⁴M³/κ — R has gone.
5. Lifetime = fuel/burn rate = 0.007 × 0.1 × Mc²/L, so t ∝ M^(1−α).

**See it in numbers**
```
synthetic main sequence (60 stars, L = M^3.5 x 0.05 dex scatter): fitted log L = 3.481 log M + +0.002
Sun: M = GM/G = 1.98848e+30 kg; t = 0.007 x 0.1 x M c^2 / L = 10.36 Gyr
t(M) = t_sun M / L = t_sun M^-2.5: 2 Msun 1.83 Gyr, 10 Msun 33 Myr, 0.5 Msun 59 Gyr
```
The fit recovers the injected slope (3.481 against 3.5) — a check of the fitting code, not of nature. The energy budget gives the Sun 10.36 Gyr. A 10 M☉ star lives 33 Myr; a 0.5 M☉ star outlives the Universe.

**Where it shows up in practice** Cluster ages from the main-sequence turn-off; problem 24.3 shows Kramers opacity changes the exponent.

### Parallax distance, distance modulus and error propagation up the ladder
**In plain words** Parallax gives distance by geometry; magnitudes convert distance to brightness; and an error in the supernova calibration multiplies every distance — and H0 — by the same factor, no matter how many supernovae you add.

**Why it is true (intuition)** Each rung measures the next rung's yardstick. A wrong yardstick is shared by everything measured with it, so averaging cannot remove it.
1. tan p = 1 au/d; for small p, d = 1/p pc by the definition of the parsec.
2. Flux ∝ 1/d², so m − M = 5 log10(d/10).
3. Differentiate d = 10^((μ+5)/5): σ_d/d = (ln 10/5) σ_μ ≈ 0.461 σ_μ.
4. If M_cal is off by δM, every dᵢ is scaled by 10^(−δM/5) and H0 = cz/d by 10^(δM/5).

**See it in numbers**
```
parallax 2.01 mas: d = 1/p = 497.5 pc (exact 1 au / tan p: rel. diff 1.1e-16); mu = 8.48
rung 1 (40 Cepheids, sigma_parallax 0.02 mas): P-L slope -2.334, zero point -4.055 (true -2.43, -4.05)
rung 2 (8 hosts at 10-40 Mpc, 25 Cepheids + 1 SN Ia each): M_SN = -19.338 (true -19.3); hosts span -19.424 .. -19.184
rung 3 (60 SNe, 0.01 < z < 0.05): H0 = 68.68 km/s/Mpc (true 70.0); calibrating on host 1 alone gives 67.24
error propagation: sigma_mu = 0.10 mag -> sigma_d/d = (ln 10 / 5) 0.10 = 0.0461; Monte Carlo 0.0461
```
ℓ Car's real 2.01 mas parallax gives 497.5 pc; the small-angle formula is exact to 1.1e-16. Rung 1 is within noise. Rung 2's M_SN (−19.338) is too bright, and rung 3 inherits it: H0 = 68.68, not 70.0 — with 60 Hubble-flow supernovae. One host alone gives 67.24.

**Where it shows up in practice** Lesson 4's `distance_modulus()`; lesson 19's Gaia cone searches (parallaxes are the ladder's first rung; Gaia data are CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO).

## Worked problems
**Problem 24.5 — ℓ Car.** *Distance:* d = 1/0.00201 arcsec = 497.5 pc — parallax first, because it is the only geometric rung. *Modulus:* μ = 5 log10(497.5) − 5 = 8.48. *Why the fractional error:* σ_p/p = 0.20/2.01 = 10 %, large enough that selection bias matters. *Absolute magnitude:* M_V = V − A_V − μ = 3.724 − 0.52 − 8.48 = −5.28 (extinction removed: dust dims regardless of distance). With the Lutz–Kelker–Hanson correction (−0.08): −5.36, matching the published −5.35 ± 0.22.

**Problem 24.7 — one supernova to H0.** *Modulus:* μ = m − M = 15.0 − (−19.3) = 34.3, using the (illustrative) calibrated M. *Distance:* 10^(34.3/5 + 1) pc = 72.44 Mpc. *Velocity:* cz = 299792.458 × 0.0165 = 4946.6 km/s — cz is fine at z this small (lesson 6). *H0:* 4946.6/72.44 = 68.3 km/s/Mpc. *Why the closing check:* if M were 0.1 mag too bright, d grows by 10^0.02 = 4.7 % and H0 falls by the same factor — theorem part (d) in one line.

## Common confusions
- **Q:** "More Hubble-flow supernovae fix a biased H0." — **A:** 60 supernovae still give 68.68; only better calibrators reduce the shared δM.
- **Q:** "L ∝ M^3.5 is a law of nature." — **A:** it is a scaling sketch with constant opacity; the fitted 3.481 only recovers what the synthetic data injected.
- **Q:** "The P–L constants in stars.py are the published calibration." — **A:** they are illustrative round numbers for a seeded simulation.
