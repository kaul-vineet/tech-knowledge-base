# Coordinates and the sky: RA/Dec to Alt/Az - is a star above the horizon now? — explained

## The intuition
A catalogue gives a star a fixed address on the sky sphere (RA, Dec). Observers need altitude and azimuth. The bridge is time: the RA on your meridian now is the local sidereal time (LST), and hour angle HA = LST − RA says how far west of the meridian the star is. One spherical-trigonometry formula turns (HA, Dec, latitude) into altitude; above 0 means "up".

**Analogy:** converting stored UTC to local display time.
Where the analogy breaks: this is a rotation of a sphere, not a constant offset.

```
UTC --julian_date()--> JD --gmst_hours()--> GMST --+lon/15--> LST
LST - RA = HA ; (HA, Dec, lat) --altaz()--> (alt, az) --alt > 0--> UP / below
```

The one thing to remember: same RA/Dec, different place or time, different altitude.

## Walkthrough: what actually happens when you run it
### Step 1 — Gurgaon at 21:00 IST
**You run** `python sky.py`

**What happens inside** `julian_date()` adds Unix seconds/86400 to 2440587.5; `altaz()` computes `sin(dec) sin(lat) + cos(dec) cos(lat) cos(ha)`; `sky_table()` sorts by altitude.

**What you see**
```
observer lat +28.46 lon +77.03 | 2026-10-29 15:30 UTC | JD 2461343.14583 | LST 23.1671 h
Deneb          56.27   310.07  UP
Polaris        28.93     0.65  UP
Sirius        -28.46    94.49  below
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** Six of ten stars are up; Polaris sits near the latitude (else check longitude and UTC).

### Step 2 — Greenwich
**You run** `python sky.py --lat 51.4778 --lon -0.0015 --utc 2026-10-29T21:00`

**What happens inside** Same code, new observer and LST.

**What you see**
```
Polaris        52.00     0.84  UP
Betelgeuse      2.33    81.02  UP
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** Polaris follows the latitude; Betelgeuse has just risen in the east.

## Common confusions
- **Q:** "Azimuth 0.65 means overhead." — **A:** azimuth is direction; altitude is height.
- **Q:** "Stellarium differs slightly, so sky.py is wrong." — **A:** J2000 positions ignore precession (~0.4 deg).
- **Q:** "IST works for --utc." — **A:** use UTC.
