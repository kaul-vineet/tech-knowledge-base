# Orbits: Kepler's laws, Kepler's equation and a planet's position — explained

## The intuition
A planet on an ellipse moves fast near the Sun and slowly far away. Kepler's trick is a "mean anomaly" M that grows evenly with time, linked to the geometry by M = E − e sin E. With no closed-form inverse, code solves for E numerically. Then three rotations give ecliptic coordinates; subtracting Earth and rotating by the obliquity gives RA/Dec.

**Analogy:** a progress bar moving at constant speed while the real download speeds up and slows down; Kepler's equation maps one to the other.
Where the analogy breaks: the mapping depends on eccentricity, and near e = 1 Newton's method needs a careful start.

```
JD --> elements(T) --> M --solve_kepler()--> E --> (x', y') in orbit plane
--3 rotations--> heliocentric xyz ; planet - Earth --obliquity--> RA/Dec
```

The one thing to remember: solve for E first; everything else is rotations.

## Walkthrough: what actually happens when you run it
### Step 1 — Kepler's equation
**You run** `python kepler.py`

**What happens inside** `solve_kepler(1.0, 0.5)` starts at M + e sin M and takes Newton steps until |dE| < 1e-12.

**What you see**
```
JD(TDB) 2461342.5 | Kepler: M=1.0 rad, e=0.5 -> E = 1.498701133518 rad
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** Degrees instead of radians would not converge.

### Step 2 — heliocentric positions
**You run** the same command (next lines).

**What happens inside** `heliocentric()` evaluates JPL's approximate elements, sets w = peri − node and M = L − peri, then rotates.

**What you see**
```
EM Bary  helio x=+0.81174 y=+0.57275 z=-0.00003 au  r=0.99346 au  period 1.0000 yr
Mars     helio x=-0.24837 y=+1.57099 z=+0.03901 au  r=1.59098 au  period 1.8808 yr
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** Earth stays within 0.983–1.017 au; Earth at 1.5 au means angles were mixed up.

### Step 3 — where to point
**You run** the same command (last lines).

**What happens inside** `geocentric_radec()` subtracts Earth and rotates about the x-axis by 23.43928 deg.

**What you see**
```
Mars     geocentric RA 139.6593 deg (9.3106 h)  Dec +17.2889 deg  distance 1.4567 au
```

**What it means / what would look wrong** JPL quotes ~40 arcsec error for Mars. Tests: `Ran 7 tests ... OK (skipped=1)` (Skyfield/DE421 in CI).

## Common confusions
- **Q:** "M is the angle from the Sun." — **A:** M is a clock angle; the true angle comes after E.
- **Q:** "Kepler's third law needs G." — **A:** in au, years and solar masses, T = a^1.5 (`period_years()`).
- **Q:** "Heliocentric xyz is already RA/Dec." — **A:** subtract Earth and rotate first.
