# Simulation-based optimisation: search, ranking & selection and fair budgets — explained

## The intuition

Every simulation evaluation is *noisy*: run design (s, Q) twice, get two costs. So "pick the lowest observed mean" has a built-in flaw. Among 81 noisy averages, the smallest is partly the best design and partly the luckiest noise draw. You pick it *because* it got lucky, so its reported cost is too optimistic — the **winner's curse**. The more designs you compare, the stronger the curse.

The fix has two stages. **Search** spends most of the budget exploring (grid, surrogate model, evolution strategy). **Selection** keeps a reserve and re-runs a short list of finalists on *fresh* random numbers — numbers that played no part in choosing them — so their means are unbiased. OCBA (Optimal Computing Budget Allocation) spends that reserve in small steps, giving more runs to finalists that are close to the leader and noisy, and few to clear losers.

Fair comparison needs **equal budgets** (`Budget` counts every simulation, raises past 324), **common random numbers** (macro-replication m uses offset 1000·m for every optimiser) and a referee: `truth.json`, 100 replications of every design on untouched streams.

Analogy: A/B testing 81 variants, shipping the best dashboard number, then watching it regress to the mean.
Where the analogy breaks: in a simulation you *can* rerun the winner on fresh traffic before you ship — that is the selection stage.

```
truth.json (100 reps × 81 designs, streams 50,000+)  ─── referee only, not in any budget
for m in 5 macro-reps:  streams offset 1000·m (CRN for all methods)
   search (264 runs) ─> top 5 ─> OCBA on fresh streams 500+ (60 runs, steps of 5) ─> pick + fresh mean
   gap = true(pick) − true(best);  bias = reported − true(pick)
```

The one thing to remember: never report the search-phase mean of the winner — re-estimate on fresh random numbers.

## Walkthrough: what actually happens when you run it

1. **You run** `python optimise.py` (shipped: RESERVE = 0).
   **What happens inside:** each optimiser runs 5 macro-replications on 324 runs; `pick()` returns the lowest search mean and reports it; scoring looks up the truth.
   **What you see:**
   ```
   81 (s, Q) designs; true best (25, 70) at 8.479/day (truth.json: 100 reps per design)
   method             mean gap worst gap  reported - true  runs
   grid_search           0.049     0.117           -0.472   324
   surrogate_search      0.277     1.085           -0.412   324
   evolution             0.052     0.145           -0.299   324
   ```
   **What it means:** all three are optimistic by 0.30–0.47 per day. The grid's worst gap (0.117) exceeds δ = 0.10.

## Failure lab: the winner's curse

1. **Inject.** `pick()` reports the winner's search-phase mean; no reserve.
2. **Symptom.** `reported - true` is negative for every method (above). Nothing errors; the dashboard just looks better than reality.
3. **Diagnose.** The bias grows with candidates and noise. **You run** `BUILD_TARGET=mine python -m unittest test_build_task test_fitness -v` → **What you see:** `Ran 5 tests in 15.004s` … `FAILED (failures=4)`; only budget fairness passes.
4. **Fix.** RESERVE = 60; re-run the top 5 on streams 500+ with OCBA in steps of 5; report the fresh mean.
5. **Proof.** **You run** `python solution/optimise.py`.
   **What you see:**
   ```
   budget 324 runs per optimiser (60 reserved for selection), 5 macro-replications
   grid_search           0.042     0.088           -0.161   324
   surrogate_search      0.090     0.186           -0.188   324
   evolution             0.088     0.241           -0.295   324
   ```
   **What it means:** the grid now meets δ in every macro-replication (worst 0.088) and its bias shrinks to −0.161. Over 5 macro-reps that is still noisy; the ADR quotes −0.143 over 12, against truth.json's own standard error of 0.085.

## The decision

ADR 0001 chose **grid search + OCBA on fresh CRN**:

- **A, grid + OCBA:** 0.042 / 0.088. Chosen: equal coverage of a small 2-D space, then an honest selection.
- **B, GP-lite surrogate + expected improvement (+ OCBA):** 0.090 / 0.186; without selection 0.277 / 1.085. Rejected *here* — 12 random starting designs leave the model uncertain in a space of only 81 points. Revisit with continuous inputs or many more designs than budget.
- **C, (μ+λ) evolution strategy (+ OCBA):** 0.088 / 0.241. Rejected for this size.

Its limit: 5 parameters × 9 levels is 59,049 designs; revisit when designs exceed about budget / 3.

**The fitness function** `test_fitness.py` enforces the brief: every optimiser ≤ 324 runs; grid mean gap ≤ 0.06 and worst ≤ 0.10 over 5 macro-reps; |reported − true| ≤ 0.15 over 12. **You run** `python -m unittest test_fitness -v` → **What you see:** `Ran 3 tests in 15.016s` … `OK`. No optimiser can win by overspending or reporting lucky numbers.

6. **You run** `python try_it.py`.
   **What you see:**
   ```
   grid + OCBA, CRN on : mean gap 0.042, worst 0.088, runs 324
   grid + OCBA, CRN off: mean gap 0.200, worst 0.372, runs 324
   evolution, budget 648     : mean gap 0.008, worst 0.021, runs 648
   ```
   **What it means:** without CRN the same method picks far worse designs — comparisons drown in noise. Doubling the evolution budget looks like a win, but it is an unfair comparison, which is exactly what the run counter prevents.

## Common confusions

**Q:** The best observed mean is an unbiased estimate of the best design. — **A:** −0.472 bias for the grid: selecting on noise biases the estimate.

**Q:** The method with the smallest gap is best. — **A:** Only at equal budgets; 648 runs vs 324 is not a method comparison.

**Q:** truth.json is cheating. — **A:** It is the referee: outside every budget, on unused streams.
