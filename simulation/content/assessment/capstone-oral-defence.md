# Capstone oral defence (L400 #22) - question bank and rubric

**Format:** 15 minutes per student (individual, even for team capstones): 3 minutes demo, 10 minutes questions, 2 minutes feedback. Two examiners. The student runs `python run_capstone.py` and `python -m unittest -v` live, and every answer must point to evidence in the repository (scorecard, ADR, model card, test). The examiners draw **4-5 questions**: at least one from each band (A theory, B evidence, C judgement).

## Question bank (10)

| # | Band | Question | What a strong answer contains |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A theory | Your DES was verified against Erlang C. Derive (or sketch) where Erlang C comes from, and say which of your model's features had to be switched off for the comparison to be fair. | Cut balance with μₙ = min(n, c)μ (Theorem 23.4); infinite patience, exponential service and a constant rate, the degenerate case of milestone M2; the pass criterion (within 0.01) set in advance. |
| 2 | A theory | Your 95% bounds come from 24 replications. Which theorem justifies them, what assumptions does it need, and how would the bound change with 96 replications? | CLT/t interval on independent replication means; finite variance; the replications are independent through spawned streams; the half-width shrinks by about 2× (1/√N). |
| 3 | A theory | Arrivals are generated by thinning. Explain why accepting candidates with probability λ(t)/λmax produces a non-homogeneous Poisson process. | Poisson axioms (Theorem 23.3); independent thinning of a Poisson process is Poisson with rate λmax·(λ(t)/λmax); λmax must bound λ(t). |
| 4 | B evidence | Show where the input model was chosen. Why lognormal, and what would an exponential assumption have done to the staffing plan? | KS D = 0.0059 from the scorecard; service variability (CV) drives waits through the Pollaczek-Khinchine intuition; with exponential service the plan would be over-staffed (L200 #7: about 25% longer waits). |
| 5 | B evidence | Patience is weakly identified (3.0-5.5 all fit). Why is it still acceptable to publish the plan? | Sensitivity: SL elasticity of only −0.01 to patience (M4); the decision is insensitive; the model card states it; holdout validation still passes. |
| 6 | B evidence | Kill a worker process during the replications and resume. Show that the results are unchanged, and explain why they would not be with position-keyed seeds. | Checkpointed runner; SeedSequence spawn keys (s, r) keyed by replication identity, not by order; L400 #20 fault. |
| 7 | B evidence | Walk me from the model card's accuracy claim to the evidence file and the test that would fail if the claim were false. | `model_card.json` → evidence JSON → `check_card()`/fitness test; hashes for code and data; the claim is generated from evidence, not typed in. |
| 8 | C judgement | Management wants to use the twin to rate individual agents. Respond as the model's owner. | Change of intended use; aggregate vs individual validity (lesson 26); ACM 1.2/2.5; AQuA proportionality; refuse or redesign with human review and appeal. |
| 9 | C judgement | Which ADR would you most want to revisit with more time or data, and what evidence would change your decision? | Names a specific ADR, its fitness function and the threshold that would trigger a revisit (e.g. demand surge > 30%, multi-skill routing). |
| 10 | C judgement | The local model's explanation contained an invented number and publication was blocked. Defend that design to a stakeholder who says "the AI is usually right". | Deterministic code decides and the model only explains; every number is checked against evidence; "usually right" is not an accuracy requirement; automation bias. |

## Rubric (per student; 100 points = 6% of the course)

| Criterion | Excellent (full) | Adequate (about half) | Inadequate (0-20%) | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Theoretical understanding | Derives or sketches the result correctly, states assumptions and links them to the code | Knows the result, shaky on assumptions | Cannot connect the model to theory | 25 |
| Evidence and reproducibility | Runs the code live and finds the evidence file, test or ADR within a minute; numbers match | Finds evidence with prompting | Cannot locate or reproduce claims | 25 |
| Judgement and limits | States the envelope, the uncertainty and what would change the decision; recognises misuse | Some limits stated | Overclaims or defers to the tool | 25 |
| Ownership of team work | Explains parts written by teammates and their trade-offs accurately | Explains own parts only | Cannot explain submitted code | 15 |
| Communication | Clear, concise, tailored to the questioner | Understandable | Confused or evasive | 10 |

A score of 40 or below in "Ownership of team work" or "Evidence and reproducibility" triggers a follow-up interview before the capstone mark is confirmed.
